Name your own price, and live with it

A seat is a named position one address holds at a time. The holder sets the price; anyone may buy at that number, at any moment, without asking.

Price it low and somebody takes it from you cheaply. Price it high and the running tax bleeds you. The honest valuation is the only comfortable one, which is the whole point — nobody has to referee it.

Tax is drawn from a prepaid escrow, which must always cover at least a week at the current price. Let the escrow run dry and the seat forecloses: its price falls to zero and the next person takes it for the cost of their own escrow.

Buying settles the outgoing holder's tax first, so arrears are never inherited. Proceeds are credited rather than pushed, so a holder whose address refuses payment cannot wedge the seat shut for everyone else.
